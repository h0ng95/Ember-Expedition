# Hybrid balance probe

Run `./tests/ExpeditionRun/run-hybrid-balance.ps1` from the project root. The defaults are seeds 1–40 and `docs/design-v0.26/hybrid-training-before.json`; `-FirstSeed`, `-Count`, and `-ReportPath` select an independent range and output. This is an automated policy comparison, not an estimate of human win rates.

Each policy starts with the same three units, 40 gold, summon price, sequential summon RNG, and encounter rewards. `final` prioritizes growth on the starting knight. Each H-01–H-06 policy reserves one member of each recipe family, grows both to E forms (including a reserved bench member), then combines them when legal. H-02 and H-05 also reserve a replacement priest before consuming their priest ingredient. After a hybrid is made, it is deployed and other growth favors an active priest, then a knight, then the remaining party; available summon gold is spent. All seven policies use these fixed priorities on both `spread` and `compact` placements. The placements are role aware, distinct, inside combat bounds, and independent of the enemies on a given wave.

`wins` is a win for that encounter with the legally grown party. After a loss the probe grants the hypothetical victory reward to measure later encounters with comparable growth; `reachWins` counts only seeds that won every encounter up to and including that wave. `meanWinSeconds` and `meanLossSeconds` are separate. `acquisitions` records each seed's first recipe families, backup priest, hybrid wave, legal operations, and final party; `sampleTraces` records the first seed's preparation actions and party for every wave. Hybrid damage/healing and its named skill count come from battle events, not inferred stats. Damage includes absorbed shields; healing is HP actually restored. One seed per policy/formation is repeated to assert deterministic output, and every preparation is captured and restored before combat.

Prioritizing a new recipe delays other growth. In particular, H-04 and H-06 can enter early boss fights with a base knight. That tradeoff is part of the fixed policy, not proof that the hybrid form itself is weak. Missing recruits and full bench opportunities are reported in acquisition records and are normal only when the underlying legal roster operations cannot obtain the target.
